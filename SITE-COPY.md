## About (first draft)

### The bottom line

After getting a bachelor’s in math and a brief failed stint as a professional poker player, I realized I needed to figure out what I actually liked to do. It was talking to people about what’s really going on with them.

So in 2010 I got a life coaching certification from the Coaches Training Institute. Then I got a master’s in psychology from John F. Kennedy University. Since then I’ve earned more than ten coaching and post-grad therapy certifications, including a PCC credential from the ICF, NARM, complex and developmental trauma therapy, couples training through Relational Life Therapy, the Couples Institute, and the Gottman Institute (although I honestly don’t think the Gottmans’ training is very good), and ACT, CBT, and psychedelic guide training through the Congregation for Sacred Practices.

Through one-on-one and one-on-two work, workshops, and corporate work, I’ve worked with over 1,000 clients and have many thousands of hours of sessions under my belt. I’ve also been featured in some fun places like NPR, Newsweek, and HuffPost and been on an assortment of podcasts, if you’re into that sort of thing.

### More about me

What’s important to know is that I both hate and love personal development. Too much of it is “leadership” and “business” coaches selling other coaches on how to become “leadership” and “business” coaches and sell “leadership” and “business” coaching to other coaches who have just gotten out of coaching school. Or scam artists implying they’ll solve all your problems in a 12k 6-month “container.”

But personal development and therapy and workshops and healing stuff are also amazing. It’s just tough that the best stuff for growing and healing is buried under woo-woo magick, dense academia, and self-responsibility bro culture. However, I can hang with woo, I love school and do jiu-jitsu like a lot of those bros. So what I do is somehow all of that (well, not the scam stuff) and none of it. The questioning of why therapy and personal growth are the way they are — and the desire for it to be fun and enjoyable — guides a lot of my practice and creations. I’m very curious about the future of healing and personal development, and fascinated by the intersection of entertainment, art, technology, and psychological practices.

I love people. I love relationships. I love trying to figure out what’s most real. And I’m obsessed with the questions of what it means to be yourself and what it means to have a successful relationship, and the practice of trying to execute these things. Like a lot of existential questions, these seem self-evident, but in my opinion, they’re really not. This obsessive, delightful search guides most of my life.

Outside of all this personal growth stuff, I’m 40 years old. I live in the Bay Area, CA. I’ve been very happily married for 12 years although it got kinda hairy there after two kids. Those two kids are now 8 (Levon) and 10 (August) and I have a dog named Luna. I love reading, writing, golf (I was lucky enough to play low-level college golf), and jiu-jitsu. In fact, I coach the local elementary school jiu-jitsu classes through a nonprofit I created. I’m learning to be a personal trainer to mix in the physical. And although I am a coach I am really a wannabe artist, and I love making stuff.

## Services (first draft)

### What coaching is

Historically, coaching asks, “Where are you now, and where do you want to go?” Whereas therapy asks, “Where are you now, and how did you get here?” I’m trained in both, but do not practice as a licensed therapist. And anyway, these days, the lines between coaching and therapy have blurred as therapy moves away from the strictly medical model and coaches bring in therapeutic education and techniques.

One way to think about coaching is it’s a place to help you figure out what you want and get you closer to it than you get on your own. In practice, it’s a guided conversation that uses inquiry, feedback, education, and skill-building to help you get to know yourself better, learn better emotional regulation skills, and feel more agency in your life to close the gap between how you want to think, feel, and act, and what you end up doing in the heat of the moment. You’ll also learn the best practices and understandings from all the fields I’ve studied, and the tools I’ve developed along the way. I use a model that I’ve created and refined over the 16 years that I’ve been in this field.

### How I do it

This type of work is often done in the repeating session model. But there’s no real reason that this is the way it has to be, other than the fact that this is the way it’s always been. However, I haven’t entirely innovated away from this yet. So I work with couples and individuals in two different ways.

**Live sessions.** These are meetings that are more like what you’re used to, on my metaphorical Zoom couch for an hour. It’s a chance to have my attention in real time with whatever’s actually happening in the moment. But I promise that we can make them feel more fun and lighthearted than you’re used to, while also making sure that we take the serious stuff seriously.

### My Practice Philosophy

Although if you ask me, I’ll definitely give you my opinion and certainly the best practices, I really don’t like the orientation that a session has to be focused on a laundry list of things that are going wrong and the incessant problems we need to solve. When we do it this way, we tend to dread our sessions and not really want to keep going.

I like it better to think of sessions or asynchronous communication as an exploration. You don’t have to come in with a problem to fix or only do it when something’s going wrong. You can bring an intention, something you want to talk about, or something you want to change. But in my model, we’re going to see what happens as you turn toward yourself. That’s usually where the good stuff is.

A lot of my work is based in a psychodynamic philosophy (jargon, I know, sorry), which means that I believe that the necessary doorways emerge when you’re guided toward them and to make space for them, that our brains and consciousness are systems that actually know how to evolve instinctively.

This does not mean I’m one of those people that think you have all the answers that you need inside yourself. The whole point of hiring me is that I’ve spent a ton of time studying all this stuff and understanding how people grow and heal. But my knowledge and skills are best utilized when we can build off of what comes up for you naturally in the midst of this type of growth experience. And when we do it this way, it becomes really fascinating and enjoyable, as opposed to something to dread.

One of the most important things to me personally and as a practitioner is the attempt to face ourselves with humility and honesty, and the search for what’s most real within and outside. I think growth starts when we stop taking ourselves so seriously, and we can inhabit that magical place between defensiveness and self-deprecation.

In a certain way, I believe that working on yourself is the process of living more and more in reality. There’s so much that we don’t know about ourselves and don’t know about the world. And instead of living in that uncertainty, we overlay our experiences with so many assumptions about ourselves and others, filling in the blanks in really poorly organized, poorly constructed ways that then become “truth.” The process of growing and developing is in a certain way the process of questioning all of that, looking in the mirror at the ways we are living in a fantasy world of exaggerated and overgeneralized negativity and positivity that cause us to loop in the same habits and feel torn and lost in the same ways over and over again, and instead living more and more in reality, which I believe leads to much greater freedom and well-being, which is kind of the whole point.
